Fabrizio Romano: the art of football journalism

How a journalist from Naples made “Here we go!” the most trusted phrase in transfer news.

October 2026— min read

  • Media
  • Psychology
Contents

    Football fans around the world know they can trust the phrase “Here we go!” when searching for transfer news. The figure behind this vast network of information is Fabrizio Romano, an Italian who came from humble beginnings and became one of the most respected football journalists in the world. With over 80 million followers across social media and numerous prestigious awards, this article recounts the story of his remarkable career and briefly examines his methods.

    From Naples to the transfer beat

    Born and raised in Naples, Romano discovered his passion for sports journalism in high school and began working for TuttoMercatoWeb, writing football articles. His career changed direction when a golden opportunity arrived. An Italian agent in Barcelona contacted him and asked him to write about two prospects from La Masia, Barcelona’s renowned academy: Gerard Deulofeu and Mauro Icardi. The latter would completely change Romano’s future.1

    The same agent later gave him information on Icardi’s transfers, first to Sampdoria and then to Inter Milan. These stories fuelled Romano’s rise and introduced him to the charm of transfer reporting. He joined Sky Sport Italia and expanded his network, meeting club officials, agents and players. For years, he has reported the progress of transfers step by step, and he rarely gets it wrong. He is revered as a trustworthy source, was named “Best Football Journalist” at the 2022 Globe Soccer Awards, and works independently while partnering with outlets such as the Guardian and CBS Sports.

    The method

    Perhaps the most important part of Romano’s success is his way of working and his dedication. His deep connections across football give him crucial and, above all, accurate information before other reporters. He is known for sleeping only around five hours a night and spending around 17 hours a day on screen. On average, he takes 50 phone calls a day and sends thousands of messages. By talking to players, agents, directors, owners and fellow journalists, he gathers information and publishes statements that are very hard to refute. His charming, energetic personality earns the trust of fans and of the football world in general.

    The key to his reliability is a method called “slow journalism.” Instead of publishing uncertain news whenever new information arrives, he releases it gradually before the final “Here we go!” post. This lowers the risk of being wrong and keeps his audience engaged by sharing the journey in small steps. The process is like serialisation: each development is shared on its own rather than in one complete story.

    When the clubs were wrong

    In some cases, his information has proved more reliable than the clubs’ own announcements. In 2015, he caught the football world’s attention with a bold claim: the Montenegrin striker Stevan Jovetić was about to leave Manchester City for Inter Milan. Manchester City denied it publicly and the Guardian doubted the report, but in the end the move went through. A more recent example came in 2022, when he predicted Marc Cucurella’s move to Chelsea. Brighton & Hove Albion explicitly rejected the news, but by August the Spanish left-back had been sold.

    Still rising

    Fabrizio Romano is at the height of his career; his audience keeps growing and so does his recognition. He has become a symbol of Italian football culture and an independent authority when it comes to confirming transfer news.
